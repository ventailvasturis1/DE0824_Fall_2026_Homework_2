Where it began

In the 19th century, German Protestant families counted down the days to Christmas by marking chalk lines on a door or lighting a candle each day. Handmade wreaths with candles and simple picture calendars followed.

The first printed calendars appeared in Germany in the early 1900s, and Gerhard Lang is usually credited with popularising them.

How advent calendars became popular

Cardboard calendars with little doors spread across Germany, then paused during the Second World War because of cardboard shortages. After the war, publishers revived them, and a widely shared 1953 photo of a US president's grandchildren with a calendar helped the idea cross the Atlantic. Chocolate behind the doors became the mass-market standard by the 1950s and 60s.

Advent calendars today

Today the calendar is no longer just for children. Adult advent calendars hide beauty products, tea, coffee, cheese, craft beer, wine, candles and socks behind the doors.

There are even "advent" calendars that contain only 12 items. They aren't strictly a count to Christmas Eve, but a "12 days" style gift set, often built around the twelve days of Christmas or a smaller, more premium selection.

  • Classic: 24 doors, 1st December to Christmas Eve
  • Adult: 24 luxury or hobby treats
  • Mini: 12 items, a "12 day" countdown

Quick facts

Typical count: 24 doors

Origin: Germany
